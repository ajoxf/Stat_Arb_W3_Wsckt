What changed in the exit engine

exit profit gate

A reversion (z) exit can no longer close below break-even. It waits until net P&L (all fees paid) ≥ the gate floor — default break-even, optionally a % of capital. The position card shows the release level as the EX chip. The dollar stop, z stop-loss, profit target and max-hold are never gated.

levels row · BE / EX / TP / SL

Every open trade shows the absolute spread values where it breaks even, releases a reversion exit, takes profit, and stops out. These are frozen at entry and don't drift with the rolling mean — once in a trade, watch the spread against these lines, not the in-trade z-score.

true-position accounting

Legs are co-sized onto whole contracts near β (lattice sizing), the actually-filled quantities are recorded at entry, and P&L is computed per leg from real fills — the dashboard matches OKX to the cent, and every stop/target acts on the position actually held.

The bet, in three words

You trade the gap, not the coins

Both legs are hedged, so market moves cancel. You only care about the spread between ETH and BTC wandering away from its average — and snapping back.

the spread

The gap (BTC-fut − β·ETH). It drifts around an average and tends to revert. Your whole P&L is this gap moving.

z-score

How unusual the gap is right now, in wiggles. 0 = average, ±2.8 = stretched (enter), ±4 = broken.

σ  ·  one wiggle

Spread std-dev, ≈37 lately. At your size that's ≈ $2 of P&L per 1σ — the ruler for every dollar below.

Five ways out — first one wins

The exits

Every tick the bot checks these in priority order (stop → take-profit → z-stop → z-exit → time). Grouped here by outcome. In practice only two fire often: Take-Profit and Dollar Stop.

Reverted — you win / scratch
Take-Profit maker Net P&L reaches the σ-fraction target — roughly half the expected snap-back (≈$3 at z 3.1). The everyday win: grab it partway home, don't wait.
Exit maker z snaps all the way back to ±0.5 — the gap is normal again, job done. (Take-Profit usually beats it to the punch.)
Max Hold maker Held 4× the spread's half-life and green and stalled (<50% reverted) → take the small profit, free the capital. Never exits into a loss.
Diverged — you lose, capped
Dollar Stop 1 maker probe → market Gap moves target ÷ R:R (≈$3.75) against you. Your primary stop. Fires one quick maker probe (saves the fee only if the book is momentarily calm — rare on a diverging stop), then markets out within ~2s to guarantee the close.
Stop-Loss market z blows out to ±4 — reversion thesis broken. Immediate market exit; speed beats fee here. (The Dollar Stop usually fires first.)
How the numbers wire together

Three rules set everything else

You don't set dollars — you set ratios. The dollars fall out of the trade's own stretch (z) and volatility (σ), which is exactly why nothing needs re-tuning at scale.

Profit target — σ-fraction 0.5

target = 0.5 · |z| · σ · qty

“Pocket half the expected snap-back.” Auto-scales with the stretch: bigger z or σ → bigger target. ≈ $3 at z 3.1 today. Only asks for half the move, so it's reachable.

Dollar stop — R:R 0.8

stop = target ÷ 0.8

The stop is derived from the target, not set separately. Lower R:R → wider stop (more room). Capped by the 1.5%-capital catastrophe ceiling (looser, so not binding).

Cost floor — 1.0× fees

target ≥ 1.0 · round-trip cost

A win must clear its own costs. The target can never sit below the ~$2.5 round-trip (fees + slippage), so a “profit” exit never fills at a net loss.

The honest catch

The stop is measured on the gross gap move, so with fees a stop really costs ~$5 net while a win nets ~$3 → breakeven win-rate ≈ 62%, and you're running ~60%.

That's why the one lever left is entry z 2.8 → ~3.1: wider stretches revert more reliably, lifting the win-rate over the line. The exit machinery is sound — the edge lives in taking only the well-stretched entries.
